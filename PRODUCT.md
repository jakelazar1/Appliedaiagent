# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Owner-operators of small service businesses: tour operators, HVAC companies, real estate agents, contractors, restaurants, landscaping services. They run lean teams, wear many hats, and lose hours every week to repetitive admin. They are not technical. They don't know what "automation" means in practice — they just know they're drowning. They are skeptical of software platforms and have been burned by tools that promised to save time but added complexity instead.

## Product Purpose

Applied AI Agent is Jake Lazar's solo practice. He builds custom AI agents for owner-operated businesses: systems that handle follow-up, scheduling, lead nurture, review requests, and other repetitive tasks by integrating with the tools the business already uses. The site's job is to earn enough trust for a prospect to book a free 15-minute demo. Nothing more. No pricing decision, no platform signup — just a conversation.

## Brand Personality

Pragmatic. Direct. Hands-on partner. Jake speaks as a fellow operator, not a vendor. Voice is first-person, specific, and credibility-forward (10 years as a winemaker is the proof that he understands owner-operated businesses from the inside). The brand is not a SaaS company or an agency. It's one expert you call.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages (Zapier, Make, Monday.com) — platform-y, feature-list-heavy
- AI hype pages (any site with "supercharge", "unlock", "transform" in the hero)
- Wine-country lifestyle aesthetics (rustic, warm amber, grape imagery) — too niche
- Big agency portfolios (cold, case-study heavy, awards-focused)
- Chatbot/widget companies — not what this is

## Design Principles

1. **Demo is the product.** Every section pushes toward one action: book the free demo. Remove anything that competes with that.
2. **Specificity over claims.** Name the industry, the problem, the result. Never say "streamline operations." Say "50% faster response time."
3. **Show Jake, not a brand.** The credibility here is a person with 10 years of domain expertise, not a logo. Voice, copy, and structure should feel like talking to Jake.
4. **Breathing room as confidence.** Whitespace signals the brand isn't trying too hard. Tight, cluttered pages feel anxious. Sparse, deliberate pages feel capable.
5. **Intelligence layer, not automation platform.** The framing is "I add a layer on top of what you already use" — not "switch to our system." This distinction must be felt in every section.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Reduced motion support required (fade-ins must degrade to instant). Color contrast ≥4.5:1 for all body text. Form labels explicit (no placeholder-only labels).
