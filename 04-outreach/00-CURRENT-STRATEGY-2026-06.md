# Outreach Strategy — Current Direction (June 2026)

This supersedes the broad 12-vertical approach in 01-OUTREACH-STRATEGY.md.
That document is now reference/archive. THIS is what we're actually running.

---

## The decision

One vertical. Email-only. Verified direct owner emails only. Quality over volume.

**Target:** Accounting & bookkeeping practices — solo CPAs, small firms,
independent bookkeepers — in Sonoma and Napa counties.

---

## Why we pivoted here (the honest reasoning)

The June 6-7 campaign sprayed 96 emails across 12 verticals in two days.
Result: 29% bounce rate, zero replies, zero conversations. The machinery
worked; the targeting didn't.

Two findings forced the pivot:

1. **The plumbing/HVAC wedge was real, but the channel wasn't.**
   Richard (stepfather, plumbing owner) described a genuine leak — shops
   install units, then have no system tracking which are due for paid annual
   maintenance, so recurring service revenue goes uncollected. Strong insight.
   BUT: plumbing/HVAC owners almost never publish a direct email. They route
   everything through contact forms and info@. So "email-only + plumbing"
   can't both be true. That mismatch is exactly why June bounced.

2. **We chose to keep the channel that fits how we want to sell.**
   Email-only, verified, drafts-to-Gmail-first is the discipline that works.
   So instead of forcing email into a vertical that hides emails, we moved to
   a vertical where owners publish their email everywhere — because email IS
   how they get clients.

Richard is an insight source, not a customer. We do not sell to family.
His pain pattern travels: it's the template for the new wedge.

---

## The wedge (reframed for accountants/bookkeepers)

Same shape as Richard's leak — untracked recurring revenue — translated:

> "You have clients paying you once a year for tax prep who should be on
> monthly bookkeeping or advisory. And clients who quietly didn't come back
> this year. Most practices your size have no system flagging either one.
> That's recurring revenue leaking out the same way a service business forgets
> its maintenance visits."

Secondary pains (for the assessment call, not the cold email):
- Tax-season intake chaos (document chase, repetitive client questions)
- Client onboarding is manual and slow
- No proactive nudge for clients drifting toward churn

Timing note: June is good. Post-tax-season, owners have bandwidth to talk.

---

## Why accountants over real estate / consultants

- **Real estate:** publishes emails, but transactional (weak recurring wedge)
  and the most AI-vendor-saturated audience there is. Skip for now.
- **Consultants/coaches:** reachable, but tiny shops, lower willingness to pay,
  and they already know their small client list cold (weak "you're not
  tracking it" hook).
- **Accountants/bookkeepers:** publish direct emails (it's their lead channel),
  strongest recurring-revenue wedge fit, June timing works. Winner.

---

## The motion

1. **Source the list** (needs a real channel — search-discovery was blocked
   the day this was written). Options:
   - Sonoma County / Napa Chamber of Commerce member directories
   - LinkedIn search: "bookkeeper" / "CPA" + Sonoma/Napa, solo & small
   - CalCPA local chapter member lists
   - Referrals from anyone in your network who uses a bookkeeper

2. **Verify each one before adding.** Visit the real site. Confirm a PUBLISHED
   direct owner email (jane@firm.com, not info@). If it's form-only or info@,
   it does NOT go on the list. This is the rule that was broken in June.

3. **Send the cold email** (see 04-EMAIL-ACCOUNTING-V1.md). Personalized,
   teammate-framed, recurring-revenue hook. Draft to Gmail first for review.

4. **Low volume, high quality.** 10-15 verified owners beats 100 guesses.
   Goal is the first real conversation, not send-count.
