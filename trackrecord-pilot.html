Call it. Seal it. Prove it.

TrackRecord is a public ledger of broker predictions. Brokers put a number on the record before a home sells — the county record grades them after.

For brokers

The brokers who call it straight can finally prove it. Every home you tour, you answer four questions; your prediction is sealed with a timestamp. When the sale records at the county, your call is graded — and your accuracy becomes an asset no marketing budget can buy. Anyone can claim they price homes right. A track record is the difference.

For sellers

Don't let a broker buy your listing with a promise your home will sell for more than it's worth. Before you sign, look up the track record: how close their past predictions landed to the county-recorded truth — every one of them, hits and misses alike.

How it works

  1. Tour. A broker tours a listed property and answers four questions: the day they toured it, the listed price, the price they predict it will sell for, and the number of days until it sells.
  2. Seal. The prediction is locked and timestamped. It cannot be edited or deleted — and it stays hidden while the listing is active, so honest calls can't disrupt live deals.
  3. Grade. When the sale is recorded, the prediction is opened and graded against the county-recorded sale price and date — public record, not self-reporting.

The rules that make it trustworthy

  • Brokers sign in with a verified email; every prediction is attributable.
  • Addresses must be picked from county parcel records — fake addresses are impossible.
  • Predictions are immutable once sealed — enforced by the database itself.
  • Sealed predictions stay hidden until the county records the sale.
  • Every graded prediction counts toward the record. No cherry-picking, no deletions.
  • Outcomes come from county-recorded sales only, source cited on every grade. Never self-reported.
  • Pilot coverage: incorporated City of Sonoma (4,091 parcels), expanding county by county.
